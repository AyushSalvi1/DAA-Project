Big-O, Big-Theta, Big-Omega

Big-O (upper bound)

f(n) = O(g(n)) means f grows no faster than a constant multiple of g for all large n. It is an upper bound, so it says nothing about the best case. Merge sort is O(n log n) because it never does better than that bound.

Big-Theta (tight bound)

f(n) = Θ(g(n)) means f grows at the same rate as g asymptotically - both an O and an Ω bound hold. Merge sort is Θ(n log n) because the bound is tight for every input.

Big-Omega (lower bound)

f(n) = Ω(g(n)) means f grows at least as fast as a constant multiple of g. It is a lower bound: linear search is Ω(1) in the best case and Ω(n) in the worst case.
