TITLE: Measuring and Improving Network Performance
CATEGORY: Computer Networks
AUTHOR: T. Banerjee
KEYWORDS: network performance, qos, latency, packet loss, jitter, throughput, load balancing

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Performance work begins with measurement. Round-trip time measures delay, packet-loss ratio measures reliability, jitter measures variation in arrival time, and goodput measures useful payload throughput after protocol overhead.

The throughput-delay product is the number of bits that can be in flight in a link at once. It is the real capacity of a path, and it explains why a link with large bandwidth but long latency cannot transfer a large file quickly.

Bottlenecks are found by measurement, not assumption: interface counters, queue depth, retransmission rate and latency percentiles across each hop. A single congested hop makes the whole path slow.

Quality of service lets a network prioritise: latency-sensitive traffic such as voice is shaped and policed before bulk transfer. Shaping smooths bursts with a queue, policing enforces a contract by dropping or marking excess.

Common remedies are content delivery networks for proximity, compression for bandwidth, caching for repeated requests, connection reuse to avoid repeated handshakes, and load balancing to spread demand. The trade-off is always between latency and consistency, and between cost and redundancy.
